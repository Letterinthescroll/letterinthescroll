The Four Verbs of Freedom

Why do we drink exactly four cups of wine?

Tap to reveal
1

They correspond to the four verbs God uses in Exodus 6:6–7 to promise redemption: "I will bring you out… I will deliver you… I will redeem you… and I will take you to me as a people." Each cup represents a different stage of the transition from slavery to nationhood.

The First National Mitzvah

What was the very first commandment given to the Israelites as a nation?

Tap to reveal
2

It wasn't the Ten Commandments! In Exodus 12:2, while still in Egypt, God commanded them to establish their own calendar (Rosh Chodesh). A slave doesn't own their own time — to be free, the first thing they had to do was take control of the clock.

The "War" Against the Nile

Why was "Blood" the very first plague?

Tap to reveal
3

The Nile was not just a river — it was a primary Egyptian deity (the god Hapi). By turning it into blood, God was "executing judgments against the gods of Egypt" (Exodus 12:12), proving that the Egyptians' source of life was under His control.

The "Rebel" Lamb

Why did God specifically choose a lamb for the Passover sacrifice?

Tap to reveal
4

The lamb was a sacred animal in Egypt (associated with the god Khnum). Tying it to their bedposts for four days and then sacrificing it was an act of incredible bravery and public rebellion against Egyptian idolatry (Exodus 8:22).

The 400-Year-Old "Luggage"

While everyone packed gold and silver, what was Moses looking for?

Tap to reveal
5

Moses spent the final moments locating the coffin of Joseph. Joseph had made his brothers swear 400 years earlier not to leave Egypt without him (Genesis 50:25). They carried his bones through the entire 40-year desert journey (Exodus 13:19).

The "Mixed Multitude"

Was it only the descendants of Jacob who left Egypt?

Tap to reveal
6

No! Exodus 12:38 mentions that a "mixed multitude" (Erev Rav) went up with them — Egyptians and other foreigners so moved by the miracles that they joined the Jewish people on their journey to freedom.

The Midday Exit

Did the Israelites "sneak out" of Egypt under the cover of night?

Tap to reveal
7

While the 10th plague struck at midnight, the Torah emphasizes they left "in the middle of the day" (Exodus 12:42). God wanted them to leave with "a high hand" — proudly and openly — as free men, not escaping like thieves.

The "No Broken Bones" Rule

What was the strangest rule about eating the original Passover lamb?

Tap to reveal
8

In Exodus 12:46, the Torah forbids breaking any of the lamb's bones. Breaking bones is how a slave eats desperately. A free person eats with dignity, leaving the bones whole to show they are not desperate for every last scrap.

The Fifth "Hidden" Expression

Why do we have a "Cup of Elijah" that no one drinks?

Tap to reveal
9

There is actually a 5th verb of redemption in Exodus 6:8: "I will bring you to the land." Since the Israelites didn't enter the land immediately, we pour a 5th cup but don't drink it — symbolizing a redemption still "in progress."

The Literal Meaning of "Pesach"

We translate Pesach as "Passover," but what does the word actually imply?

Tap to reveal
10

While it can mean "to skip," in Isaiah 31:5 the root means "to shield" or "to protect." God wasn't just skipping the houses — He was actively standing over the doors to shield the families inside from the plague.
