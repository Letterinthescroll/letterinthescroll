Potato Knishes from Scratch

Crispy, golden-baked pastries with a thin, flaky dough wrapped around a rich filling of mashed potatoes and caramelized onions. A New York Jewish deli classic made at home.

Prep Time1 hr 30 min (including resting)
Cook Time35 min
Total Time2 hrs 5 min
Servings12 knishes
DifficultyMedium
Year-Round Comfort Food

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • For the dough:
  • 2.5 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 large egg
  • 1/4 cup schmaltz or neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup warm water
  • 1 tsp white vinegar
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • For the filling:
  • 3 lbs Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and quartered
  • 2 large onions, diced
  • 3 tbsp schmaltz or butter
  • 1 large egg
  • Salt and white pepper to taste
  • Egg wash: 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp water

Directions

  1. Make the dough first, as it needs to rest. In a bowl, combine the flour, baking powder, and salt. In a separate bowl, whisk together the egg, schmaltz (or oil), warm water, and vinegar. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and mix until a soft dough forms. Knead on a lightly floured surface for 3 to 4 minutes until smooth and elastic. Wrap in plastic and rest for 1 hour at room temperature.
  2. While the dough rests, prepare the filling. Boil the quartered potatoes in salted water until completely tender, about 18 to 20 minutes. Drain and mash thoroughly — no lumps.
  3. In a skillet, cook the diced onions in schmaltz (or butter) over medium heat for 12 to 15 minutes until deeply golden and caramelized. Add the caramelized onions and 1 egg to the mashed potatoes. Season generously with salt and white pepper. Mix well and let cool to room temperature.
  4. Preheat the oven to 375°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  5. Divide the rested dough in half. On a well-floured surface, roll one half into a large, thin rectangle (approximately 12 by 18 inches). The dough should be thin enough that you can almost see through it.
  6. Spoon half the potato filling in a continuous log along one long edge of the dough rectangle, about 2 inches from the edge. Roll the dough over the filling like a jelly roll, creating a long, filled tube. Seal the long seam by pressing the dough together.
  7. Using the side of your finger or a chopstick, press down across the tube every 3 inches to pinch and separate it into individual knishes. Cut between the pinched sections with a knife. Tuck the open ends underneath each knish.
  8. Place the knishes seam-side down on the baking sheet. Repeat with the remaining dough and filling. Brush the tops generously with egg wash.
  9. Bake for 35 minutes until the knishes are deeply golden brown and crispy. Let cool for 5 minutes before serving. They are best eaten warm with spicy mustard on the side.
Chef's Tip

The vinegar in the dough is a baker's trick — it relaxes the gluten and makes the dough extraordinarily easy to roll thin without snapping back. The thinner the dough, the better the ratio of crispy crust to creamy filling.
