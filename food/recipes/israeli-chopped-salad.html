Authentic Israeli Chopped Salad

The freshest, crunchiest salad in the world — finely diced cucumber and tomato dressed with nothing but lemon juice, olive oil, and fresh herbs. The cornerstone of every Israeli meal.

Prep Time15 min
Cook Time0 min
Total Time15 min
Servings4
DifficultyEasy
Year-Round Vegan Gluten-Free

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • 1 lb Persian or seedless cucumbers (about 6 small), finely diced
  • 1 lb firm, ripe tomatoes (about 4 medium), finely diced
  • 1/2 medium red onion, very finely minced
  • 1/2 cup fresh flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp fresh mint, finely chopped
  • 3 tbsp freshly squeezed lemon juice (about 1.5 lemons)
  • 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste

Directions

  1. The key to this salad is the cut. Every piece of cucumber and tomato should be diced to a uniform, very small size — roughly 1/4 inch cubes. This uniform dice ensures that every bite contains a perfect ratio of cucumber to tomato, and that the dressing coats every piece evenly. This is not a rough chop — it is a precision exercise.
  2. Using a sharp chef's knife, dice the cucumbers into 1/4-inch pieces. If using regular cucumbers, peel them and remove the seeds first. Persian cucumbers can be used as-is.
  3. Dice the tomatoes to the same 1/4-inch size. Use firm, ripe tomatoes — tomatoes that are too soft will turn to mush when diced this small and release too much liquid. Remove any excess liquid and seeds from the cutting board.
  4. Mince the red onion very finely — the pieces should be even smaller than the cucumber and tomato, almost invisible. Too-large onion pieces will dominate the bite.
  5. Combine the diced cucumbers, tomatoes, and minced onion in a large glass bowl. Add the chopped parsley and mint.
  6. Just before serving — this is important, not 20 minutes before, not while preparing other dishes — whisk together the lemon juice and olive oil. Pour the dressing over the salad, season with salt and pepper, and toss gently to combine.
  7. Serve immediately. Israeli chopped salad does not hold — the salt draws moisture from the vegetables within minutes, and after 15 to 20 minutes it becomes watery and loses its crunch. Make it last, dress it last, and eat it first.
Chef's Tip

Dress this salad at the very last possible moment before serving. The salt pulls water out of the cucumbers and tomatoes, turning a crispy salad into a watery soup within 15 to 20 minutes. Use the firmest tomatoes you can find.
