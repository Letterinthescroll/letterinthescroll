Homemade Labneh with Za'atar

Thick, tangy strained yogurt drowned in green olive oil and showered with fragrant za'atar. The simplest and most satisfying appetizer or breakfast spread on the Israeli table.

Prep Time10 min
Cook Time0 min (24-48 hrs straining)
Total Time24-48 hrs
Servings6
DifficultyEasy
Year-Round Dairy Gluten-Free

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • 32 oz (2 lbs) full-fat Greek yogurt or plain whole-milk yogurt
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • High-quality extra virgin olive oil (generous amount for serving)
  • 2 tbsp za'atar spice blend
  • Optional toppings: fresh mint, toasted pine nuts, cherry tomatoes, olives
  • Warm pita bread for serving

Directions

  1. In a bowl, mix the yogurt and salt together until the salt is evenly distributed.
  2. Set a fine-mesh strainer or colander over a deep bowl. Line it with 3 layers of cheesecloth, leaving enough overhang to gather and tie the edges. Pour the salted yogurt into the center of the cheesecloth.
  3. Gather the edges of the cheesecloth and tie them together securely, wrapping them around a wooden spoon or chopstick. Suspend the spoon over the rim of a tall container (such as a large pitcher or pot) so the yogurt hangs freely without touching the bottom. The whey will drip out slowly into the container below.
  4. Place the entire setup in the refrigerator. Let the yogurt strain for 24 hours for a spreadable consistency (similar to cream cheese), or up to 48 hours for a very thick, moldable labneh that can be rolled into balls.
  5. After straining, unwrap the cheesecloth. The labneh will be thick, dense, and tangy — roughly half the volume of the original yogurt. Discard the collected whey (or save it for smoothies or bread-baking).
  6. To serve, spread the labneh in a shallow bowl or plate using the back of a large spoon, creating a swirling, swooshed pattern with a well in the center. Drown it generously in high-quality olive oil — be generous, as the oil is an essential part of the dish, not just a garnish. Sprinkle za'atar heavily over the entire surface.
  7. Add any desired toppings: a few fresh mint leaves, a handful of toasted pine nuts, halved cherry tomatoes, or a few olives. Serve with warm, fresh pita bread for tearing and scooping.
Chef's Tip

The quality of labneh depends on two things: full-fat yogurt and patience. Low-fat yogurt produces a thin, watery result. The longer you strain, the thicker it gets. For labneh balls (a traditional preservation method), strain for 48 hours, roll into 1-inch balls, and store submerged in olive oil in a jar.
