Syrian Lachmagine (Tamarind Meat Pies)

Thin, crispy rounds of dough topped with a tangy, tamarind-laced meat mixture, garnished with pine nuts and pomegranate seeds. A Syrian-Jewish party essential.

Prep Time30 min + 2 hrs chill
Cook Time18 min
Total Time2 hrs 48 min
Servings24 pieces
DifficultyMedium
Year-Round Party Food

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • 24 mini pizza dough rounds (store-bought, or use the challah dough rolled thin)
  • 1 lb ground beef (85% lean)
  • 1 cup tamarind sauce (oot) or 3 tbsp tamarind paste mixed with 3/4 cup water
  • 4 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1.5 tsp ground allspice
  • 1.5 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 medium onion, very finely minced
  • Pine nuts for topping
  • Fresh pomegranate seeds for garnish
  • Tahini for serving

Directions

  1. In a large bowl, combine the ground beef, minced onion, tamarind sauce, tomato paste, lemon juice, allspice, salt, and pepper. Mix thoroughly by hand, kneading and squeezing the mixture for 2 to 3 minutes until it becomes cohesive and sticky — this ensures the meat adheres to the dough during baking and does not crumble. Cover and refrigerate for at least 2 hours (or overnight) to let the flavors develop.
  2. Preheat your oven to 350°F. Line two baking sheets with parchment paper.
  3. Place the dough rounds on the prepared baking sheets. Take about 1 tablespoon of the meat mixture and spread it evenly over each dough round, pressing it firmly to the very edges. The meat should form a thin, uniform layer that covers the entire surface. This is important because the meat shrinks during baking — if you do not press it to the edges, you will end up with bare dough around the rim.
  4. Press a few pine nuts into the meat topping on each piece.
  5. Bake for 15 to 18 minutes until the dough edges are golden and crispy and the meat is cooked through. The lachmagine should be crisp enough to hold when picked up but still pliable enough to fold.
  6. Remove from the oven and immediately garnish with fresh pomegranate seeds. Serve warm with a drizzle of tahini on the side.
Chef's Tip

Press the meat mixture all the way to the very edges of the dough — it shrinks significantly during baking. The 2-hour refrigeration allows the tamarind to penetrate the meat and develop the tangy, complex flavor that defines this dish.
