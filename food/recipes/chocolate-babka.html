Uri Scheft's Chocolate Babka

A towering, laminated babka with swirls of Nutella and dark chocolate chips running through a rich, buttery brioche-like dough, finished with a sticky sugar syrup glaze.

Prep Time1 hr + overnight chill
Cook Time30 min
Total Time6+ hrs (including chilling and rising)
Servings2 loaves
DifficultyAdvanced
Shabbat Shavuot

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • For the dough:
  • 4.5 cups (575g) all-purpose flour
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup (100g) granulated sugar
  • 120g (1 stick + 1 tbsp) unsalted butter, cut into 1/2-inch cubes, at room temperature
  • 170ml (3/4 cup) whole milk, at room temperature
  • 30g fresh yeast (or 2 1/4 tsp instant yeast)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • For the chocolate filling:
  • 1.5 cups Nutella or chocolate-hazelnut spread
  • 1 cup dark chocolate chips (60-70% cacao)
  • For the sugar syrup:
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup water

Directions

  1. In the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the dough hook, combine the flour, eggs, sugar, milk, and yeast. Mix on low speed for 2 minutes until a shaggy dough forms, then increase to medium speed and knead for 5 minutes until the dough is smooth and pulls away from the sides of the bowl.
  2. With the mixer running on medium speed, begin adding the butter one cube at a time. Wait for each cube to be fully incorporated before adding the next — this process takes about 8 to 10 minutes total. The dough will look like it is falling apart after the first few additions; this is normal. Continue kneading patiently and it will come together into a smooth, glossy, elastic dough that pulls cleanly away from the bowl. Add the salt during the last minute of kneading.
  3. Wrap the dough tightly in plastic wrap and refrigerate for a minimum of 2 hours and ideally overnight (up to 12 hours). The cold rest firms the butter, making the dough much easier to roll out thinly, and allows the gluten to relax.
  4. Remove the chilled dough from the refrigerator. Divide it in half. Working with one half at a time on a well-floured surface, roll the dough into a large rectangle approximately 18 inches wide by 9 inches tall. The dough should be about 1/4 inch thick.
  5. Spread half of the Nutella evenly over the entire surface of the rectangle, going all the way to the edges. Scatter half of the chocolate chips over the Nutella layer.
  6. Starting from the long (18-inch) side, roll the dough into a very tight cylinder, keeping it as even as possible. Pinch the seam closed. Using a sharp serrated knife, cut the cylinder in half lengthwise down the entire length — you will now have two long strips with the chocolate layers fully exposed.
  7. Lay the two strips side by side, cut sides facing up. Cross one strip over the other to form an X. Then twist the two halves together, keeping the cut sides facing upward as much as possible to showcase the chocolate swirl layers.
  8. Carefully transfer the twisted babka into a greased 9x5-inch loaf pan. Repeat the entire process with the second half of dough.
  9. Cover the pans loosely with oiled plastic wrap and let the babkas rise at room temperature for approximately 2 hours until the dough has risen to about 1 inch above the rim of the pans and looks puffy.
  10. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Bake the babkas for 30 minutes, rotating the pans halfway through, until the tops are deep golden brown and the internal temperature reaches 190°F.
  11. While the babkas bake, make the sugar syrup. Combine the sugar and water in a small saucepan and bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves. Let it boil for 1 minute, then remove from heat.
  12. As soon as the babkas come out of the oven, brush them generously with the hot sugar syrup — use all of it. The syrup soaks into the hot bread and keeps the babka incredibly moist for days. Let the babkas cool in their pans for 10 minutes, then turn them out onto a wire rack to cool completely.
Chef's Tip

Adding the butter cube by cube is the most tedious part, but it is what creates the laminated, pull-apart layers that make babka so special. Chilling overnight makes the dough dramatically easier to handle. The sugar syrup applied immediately after baking is what keeps the babka moist — do not skip it.
