Fluffy Matzah Ball Soup

Crystal-clear golden chicken broth with cloud-like matzah balls that practically dissolve on the tongue. The undisputed centerpiece of the Passover Seder table, but beloved all year round.

Prep Time30 min + 1 hr chill
Cook Time2 hrs 45 min
Total Time4 hrs 15 min
Servings8
DifficultyMedium
Passover Shabbat Year-Round

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • For the broth:
  • 1 whole chicken (4-5 lbs), cut into pieces
  • 2 large onions, halved (skin on for color)
  • 6 large carrots, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 4 parsnips, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 4 celery stalks with leaves
  • 1 small bunch fresh dill
  • 1 small bunch fresh parsley
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 14 cups cold water
  • For the matzah balls:
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup matzo meal (regular, not cake meal)
  • 1/4 cup chicken stock or broth
  • 1/4 cup schmaltz (rendered chicken fat) or neutral oil
  • 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • 1/2 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp seltzer water (optional, for extra fluffiness)

Directions

  1. Begin with the broth, which needs at least 2 hours of simmering. Place the chicken pieces in a very large stockpot and cover with 14 cups of cold water. Starting with cold water and bringing it slowly to a boil extracts the maximum flavor and gelatin from the chicken and bones, resulting in a richer, more full-bodied broth.
  2. Set the pot over high heat and bring to a boil. As the water heats, impurities will rise to the surface as a gray, foamy scum. Using a large spoon or fine-mesh skimmer, skim this foam off diligently for the first 10 to 15 minutes. This skimming step is what produces a clear, golden broth rather than a cloudy one.
  3. Once the foam has subsided, add the halved onions (leave the skins on — they contribute a beautiful golden color to the broth), carrots, parsnips, celery, dill, parsley, salt, and peppercorns. Reduce the heat to maintain the gentlest possible simmer — you should see lazy bubbles rising slowly to the surface, not a rolling boil.
  4. Simmer the broth uncovered for 2 hours, adjusting the heat as needed to maintain that gentle simmer. The broth will reduce slightly and turn a deep, rich gold. After 2 hours, remove the pot from the heat. Strain the broth through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean pot, pressing gently on the solids to extract all the liquid. Discard the chicken and vegetables (or save the chicken meat for another use). Let the broth cool slightly, then refrigerate until the fat solidifies on the surface. Remove and discard the fat (or save it — this is schmaltz).
  5. While the broth simmers, prepare the matzah ball mixture. In a mixing bowl, whisk the eggs until frothy. Add the chicken stock, schmaltz (or oil), nutmeg, ground ginger, and salt. Whisk to combine. Fold in the matzo meal gently with a spatula — do not beat or overmix, as this develops gluten and produces dense, heavy matzah balls. If using seltzer for extra fluffiness, fold it in last. The mixture will be wet and soft.
  6. Cover the bowl tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for a minimum of 1 hour, and up to 4 hours. This rest is essential — the matzo meal needs time to fully absorb the liquid, which gives the matzah balls their structure and prevents them from falling apart in the water.
  7. When you are ready to cook the matzah balls, bring a very large pot of well-salted water to a gentle boil. Wet your hands with cold water and gently form the mixture into balls about the size of golf balls (roughly 1.5 inches in diameter). Do not pack them tightly — a light touch is the secret to fluffy matzah balls. You should get about 12 balls.
  8. Carefully drop the balls into the gently boiling water. Cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid, reduce the heat to a simmer, and cook for 45 minutes. Do not lift the lid during this entire time — the steam trapped inside is what makes the matzah balls expand and become light and fluffy. Peeking lets the steam escape and results in dense, heavy balls.
  9. To serve, reheat the strained broth until steaming hot. Place 1 or 2 matzah balls in each bowl, ladle the hot broth over them, and add a few pieces of fresh carrot (cook them separately in the broth for 15 minutes before serving if desired). Garnish with fresh dill fronds.
Chef's Tip

For the fluffiest possible matzah balls, fold in 1 tablespoon of seltzer water at the end and use a very light hand when forming the balls. The number one rule: do not lift the lid during the 45-minute cooking time. The steam is doing all the work.
