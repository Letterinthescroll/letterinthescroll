Iraqi Kubbeh Bamia (Okra Soup)

Semolina dumplings stuffed with spiced meat, simmered in a sweet-and-sour okra and tomato broth. A beloved variation in the Iraqi kubbeh soup family.

Prep Time1 hr
Cook Time45 min
Total Time1 hr 45 min
Servings8
DifficultyAdvanced
Shabbat Year-Round

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • For the dumplings:
  • 3 cups fine semolina
  • 1/4 cup neutral oil
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 lb ground beef, 1 onion (minced), 1 tsp baharat spice blend, salt and pepper (for filling)
  • For the soup:
  • 1 lb fresh or frozen okra, trimmed
  • 2 medium onions, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3/4 cup fresh lemon juice (about 4 lemons)
  • 4 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 6 cups water
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tsp salt

Directions

  1. Begin by preparing the soup base. Heat 2 tablespoons of oil in a large soup pot over medium heat. Add the diced onions and cook for 5 minutes until softened. Add the minced garlic and stir for 1 minute. Add the tomato paste and cook for another minute, stirring to caramelize slightly.
  2. Add the okra (if using fresh, leave them whole; if frozen, add directly without thawing). Cook for 5 minutes, stirring gently to avoid breaking the okra apart.
  3. Add 6 cups of water, the lemon juice, sugar, and salt. The sweet-sour balance is the hallmark of this soup — taste and adjust as needed. Bring to a gentle boil.
  4. While the soup base comes together, prepare the dumplings using the same technique described in recipe 13 (Kubbeh Hamusta). Mix the semolina dough with oil, water, and salt. Brown the ground beef with onion and baharat for the filling. With wet hands, form walnut-sized balls of dough, press a well in the center, fill with meat, and seal into patties.
  5. Drop the kubbeh dumplings into the simmering soup 5 at a time, waiting 30 seconds between each addition. Reduce heat to a gentle simmer, cover, and cook for 30 minutes.
  6. The soup is ready when the okra is completely tender, the dumplings are cooked through and slightly swollen, and the broth has a thick, slightly viscous texture from the okra's natural thickening properties. Adjust the lemon-sugar balance one final time before serving.
Chef's Tip

Keep the okra whole when adding to the pot — cutting okra releases its mucilaginous interior, which will make the soup overly thick and slimy. Whole okra thickens the broth gently and maintains a pleasant texture.
