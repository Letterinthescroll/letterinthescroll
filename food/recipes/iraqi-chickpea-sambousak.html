Iraqi Chickpea Sambousak

Golden fried turnovers with a filling of mashed chickpeas, deeply caramelized onions, and turmeric. A highlight of the Iraqi Hanukkah and Purim tables.

Prep Time45 min
Cook Time45 min
Total Time1 hr 30 min
Servings24 pieces
DifficultyMedium
Hanukkah Purim Fried

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • For the dough:
  • 1/2 lb (225g) all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp granulated sugar
  • 2/3 cup warm water (110°F)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • For the filling:
  • 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • For frying:
  • Neutral oil (canola or vegetable), about 2 cups
  • 2 small carrots, peeled (for the oil)

Directions

  1. Start by making the dough. In a large mixing bowl, combine the flour, instant yeast, sugar, and salt. Make a well in the center and pour in the warm water and olive oil. Using a wooden spoon, stir from the center outward until a shaggy dough forms. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and knead firmly for 5 minutes, pushing the dough away from you with the heel of your hand, folding it back over itself, and rotating it a quarter turn. The dough should become smooth, slightly tacky, and spring back when poked with a finger.
  2. Shape the dough into a ball and place it in a lightly oiled bowl, turning once to coat all sides. Cover the bowl tightly with plastic wrap and place it in a warm, draft-free spot (such as an oven that has been briefly heated to 200°F and then turned off). Let the dough rise for 1 full hour until it has doubled in size and feels pillowy and light.
  3. While the dough rises, prepare the filling. Drain and rinse the chickpeas thoroughly. Place them in the bowl of a food processor and pulse 6 to 8 times until you achieve a chunky, crumbly texture — similar to the consistency of a rough tuna salad. Do not over-process into a smooth hummus-like paste; the textural contrast is essential to a good sambousak.
  4. Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the finely diced onion and cook, stirring occasionally, for 10 to 12 minutes until the onion is deeply golden and caramelized. The slow caramelization brings out the natural sweetness of the onion, which will balance the earthiness of the chickpeas.
  5. Add the turmeric, salt, and pepper to the caramelized onions and stir for 30 seconds to toast the spice. Add the pulsed chickpeas to the skillet and mix everything together thoroughly. Cook the mixture for 2 minutes, stirring constantly, to allow the flavors to meld. Taste and adjust the salt. Transfer the filling to a plate and let it cool to room temperature before assembling.
  6. Once the dough has risen, punch it down gently and turn it out onto a lightly floured surface. Divide the dough into 24 equal pieces (each about the size of a large marble). Roll each piece into a ball between your palms, then use a rolling pin to flatten each ball into a circle roughly 4 inches in diameter. Try to keep the center of each circle slightly thicker than the edges — this prevents the filling from bursting through during frying.
  7. Place 1 level tablespoon of the cooled chickpea filling in the center of each dough circle. Fold the dough in half over the filling to create a half-moon shape. Press the edges firmly together with your fingertips, then crimp the sealed edge by pressing with the tines of a fork. This both decorates the sambousak and ensures a tight seal that will not open during frying.
  8. Pour the neutral oil into a large, deep skillet to a depth of about 1/4 inch. Add the peeled carrots to the oil — this is a traditional Iraqi technique that keeps the frying oil clear and prevents it from burning and smoking too quickly. Heat the oil over medium-high heat until it reaches 350°F (a small piece of dough dropped in should immediately sizzle and float to the surface).
  9. Fry the sambousak in batches of 4 to 5 at a time, being careful not to crowd the pan. Cook for about 1 minute per side, flipping once with a slotted spoon, until both sides are an even, deep golden brown. Remove to a wire rack set over a baking sheet and let them drain for a minute. Serve warm. These are best eaten within a few hours of frying, though they can be reheated in a 350°F oven for 8 minutes.
Chef's Tip

Frying the carrots in the oil alongside the turnovers is a time-honored Iraqi technique — the carrots absorb impurities and sugar from the dough that would otherwise burn and darken the oil. Replace the carrots if they blacken.
