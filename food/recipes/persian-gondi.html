Persian Gondi (Chickpea Dumplings)

Cardamom-scented chicken and chickpea flour dumplings simmered gently in golden broth. The quintessential Persian Shabbat starter, served in nearly every Iranian Jewish home on Friday nights.

Prep Time30 min + 2 hrs chill
Cook Time2 hrs
Total Time4 hrs 30 min
Servings8
DifficultyMedium
Shabbat Gluten-Free Option

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • 1 lb ground chicken breast
  • 1/2 lb ground dark turkey
  • 1 1/2 cups chickpea flour (besan)
  • 2 medium onions, roughly chopped
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • For the broth:
  • 8 cups rich homemade chicken broth
  • 1 cup whole cooked chickpeas
  • 2 carrots, peeled and halved
  • 1 small turnip, peeled and quartered
  • Fresh basil and mint for serving

Directions

  1. Begin by preparing the onion puree. Place the roughly chopped onions in a food processor and process until you have a smooth, liquid puree. You want this to be completely smooth with no chunks remaining, as any onion pieces will cause the dumplings to have an uneven texture and may cause them to crack during cooking.
  2. In a large mixing bowl, combine the ground chicken, ground turkey, chickpea flour, pureed onion, oil, ground cardamom, turmeric, salt, and pepper. Using your hands or a sturdy wooden spoon, mix and knead the mixture for a full 2 minutes until it becomes completely unified and cohesive. The texture should feel like soft, slightly sticky playdough — it should be firm enough to hold a clear thumbprint when you press your finger into it, but soft enough that you could cut through it easily with a spoon.
  3. If the mixture feels too wet and loose, add chickpea flour 1 tablespoon at a time until it reaches the right consistency. If it feels too dry and crumbly, add a teaspoon of water at a time. Cover the bowl tightly with plastic wrap and refrigerate for a minimum of 2 hours, and ideally 4 hours or overnight. This resting period is not optional — the chickpea flour needs time to hydrate and absorb the moisture from the meat and onion, which is what gives the dumplings their characteristic structure and prevents them from falling apart in the broth.
  4. When you are ready to cook, bring the chicken broth to a gentle boil in a large, wide pot. Add the whole chickpeas, carrot halves, and turnip quarters to the broth.
  5. Remove the gondi mixture from the refrigerator. Wet your hands thoroughly with cold water (this prevents sticking) and form the mixture into balls about 1.5 inches in diameter — roughly the size of a golf ball. You should get approximately 12 to 14 dumplings. Place them on a plate as you work, keeping your hands wet at all times.
  6. Gently lower the dumplings into the simmering broth one at a time, spacing them apart so they have room to expand (they will nearly double in size). Reduce the heat to maintain a very gentle simmer — vigorous boiling will break the dumplings apart. Cook uncovered for 1.5 to 2 hours. The dumplings are done when they have expanded noticeably, float easily, and are tender and cooked through when you cut one in half to test.
  7. Serve the gondi in shallow bowls with plenty of the golden broth, a few chickpeas, and pieces of the cooked carrot and turnip. Garnish with fresh basil and mint leaves torn over the top.
Chef's Tip

The refrigeration step is non-negotiable. Without it, the chickpea flour has not hydrated, and your dumplings will disintegrate in the broth. Test one dumpling first — if it falls apart, knead in a bit more chickpea flour and chill for another 30 minutes before proceeding with the rest.
