Zaalouk (Moroccan Eggplant & Tomato Dip)

A smoky, spiced dip of roasted eggplant mashed with tomatoes, cumin, paprika, and fresh cilantro. Served at room temperature with crusty bread, it is an essential part of the Moroccan mezze spread.

Prep Time15 min
Cook Time55 min
Total Time1 hr 10 min
Servings6
DifficultyEasy
Year-Round Vegan Gluten-Free

Ingredients

Adjust servings:
  • 2 lbs eggplant (about 2 large), peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 3 large ripe tomatoes, peeled and roughly chopped (or 1 can 14 oz diced tomatoes)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 cup extra virgin olive oil, divided
  • 1.5 tsp ground cumin
  • 1.5 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1/4 cup fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
  • Crusty bread or pita for serving

Directions

  1. Preheat your oven to 450°F. Toss the eggplant cubes with 2 tablespoons of the olive oil, spreading them in a single layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet. Roast for 30 to 35 minutes, tossing once halfway through, until the cubes are deeply browned on the edges and completely soft throughout. The high-heat roasting concentrates the eggplant's flavor and removes excess moisture that would otherwise make the dip watery.
  2. While the eggplant roasts, prepare the tomato base. Heat the remaining olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the minced garlic and cook for 1 minute until fragrant. Add the chopped tomatoes (or canned diced tomatoes, drained of excess liquid), smoked paprika, cumin, cayenne (if using), and salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 15 minutes until the tomatoes break down into a thick, concentrated sauce.
  3. Add the roasted eggplant to the skillet. Using a fork or potato masher, mash the eggplant into the tomato sauce to your desired consistency — some people prefer it chunky and rustic, while others like it smoother. Either way, it should not be completely smooth like hummus.
  4. Continue cooking the combined mixture over medium-low heat for 10 more minutes, stirring occasionally, to allow the flavors to meld. The dip should be thick enough to hold its shape on a spoon.
  5. Remove from the heat and stir in the lemon juice and chopped cilantro. Taste and adjust the salt, lemon, and cayenne to your preference.
  6. Transfer to a shallow serving bowl and let cool to room temperature. Zaalouk is traditionally served at room temperature, not hot, as the flavors become more pronounced as it cools. Serve with crusty bread or warm pita for scooping.
Chef's Tip

Roasting the eggplant at high heat rather than sauteing it gives zaalouk a smoky depth that cannot be replicated by stovetop cooking. The dip tastes even better the next day — the spices continue to develop as it sits in the refrigerator.
